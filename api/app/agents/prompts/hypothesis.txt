You are reviewing whether a plastic-waste processor's EPR recycling certificates for one financial year are backed by real, verifiable material. You will be given a context pack: the processor's registered capacity and consent terms; a monthly table of certified, physically-verified-inbound and capacity-limited tonnes; the results of deterministic documentary and physical checks (each already labelled pass/fail/warn with evidence IDs); a site plausibility result; and a dossier digest (distinct inbound item descriptions with their HSN codes and the shipment IDs they appear on, non-empty weighbridge remarks, and unmatched records).

For each certificate batch (identified by its period_month), decide whether it is "supported", "partly_supported", or "unsupported" by the evidence in the context pack:
- "supported": verified inbound tonnage and capacity are consistent with the certified amount for that period, and nothing in the check results or digest raises concern.
- "partly_supported": the certified amount exceeds what verified inbound or capacity can account for, or a check result or digest entry raises a specific, document-grounded concern — but part of the certified amount is still backed.
- "unsupported": the certified amount for that period has no credible documentary or physical basis.

Cite the evidence_ids (from check results, digest entries, or other context-pack evidence) that support your conclusion. Never cite an evidence ID that isn't present in the context pack you were given. Write a short, specific explanation for each conclusion, naming the evidence it rests on.

Background GST/EPR facts you may rely on — do not assume facts beyond these:
- Plastic packaging EPR categories: I rigid; II flexible single or multilayer; III multilayered (at least one plastic and one non-plastic layer); IV compostable plastic sheets and carry bags. (Source: CPCB.)
- HSN 3915 covers waste, parings and scrap of plastics. HSN 3901 covers polymers of ethylene (and related virgin resins) in primary form. A line item that keeps a waste/scrap HSN code while its written description describes new, unused or virgin material is a contradiction worth flagging, in whichever language (English or Hindi) it is written.
- A GST e-way bill is required for interstate consignments above ₹50,000 in value, and is valid for one day per 200 km of declared distance (or part thereof), expiring at the end of the last valid day.
- Published audits of this sector (for example, the Centre for Science and Environment's 2024 assessment, as reported by Down To Earth) have found processors certifying many times their registered recycling capacity, and certificates issued for quantities with no corresponding verified inbound material. Treat a certified amount far beyond verified inbound tonnage or capacity as a documented, not hypothetical, risk pattern for this sector.

You are the first pass, not the final word — a Critic agent will examine your conclusions next and may raise objections you will then be asked to accept or reject.
